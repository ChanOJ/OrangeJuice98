Q> Why so many cookies in your sites ?
R> Opposed to what magazines often seem to say, cookies are only "dangerous" or something like that because they are runned in your back. An important thing to know is, the cooky can ONLY be read by the site it is on ! So NO other can read the Orange Juice's cookies. But why are there cookies on the it ? First, there aren't that many, they are only used to display the original (or grabbed, who said "ripped" !?! :-) images in the parties reports. Actually, to display a centered picture on a black background with a link to the previous page, one html file is required for each image. Considering the number of images, an alternate solution has been used: the image name is written in a cookie, the new loaded page (view_img.htm) gets this cookie and displays the wanted image on a black background, centered, etc. Then the cookie is immediately deleted (no use any more). But I use now another JavaScript function, cleaner and more efficiency. So, cookies will soon totally disappear.

Some sites (Altavista for example) found a way to cheat the cookies limitation; they use advertising banners. Actually these banners are hosted by a main site (just check the source to get it's name) which puts in place and reads the cookies, then it sends all these informations to the scripts of the site you're surfing on. The most efficient way to get rid of commercial cookies is to delete them at eatch launch of your browser through a batch file. It will take you about 5 minutes to be efficiantly protected, and will even enable site like Orange Juice, which do a lite and honest use of this invention, to work properly.

Q> What is OJ's update frequency ?
R> There is no predefined timing. As soon as there is something new or to change, we change it, and we translate it (note: with a little delay ;-).

Q> Why are some images compressed in the gfx compo's reports ?
R> When clicked on the image preview, the original image made by the graphist appears. Because the graphist draws the picture pixel by pixel, it has to be kept so, and has not to be modified ! (we switch directly from LBM, IFF or PCX to GIF, without any retouch). In the case of a 24 bytes image, the JPG compression is tested, if changes are visible, the gfx is compressed in a RAR file. That way, downloadable gfx compos on Orange Juice are of equal quality as on FTP or BBS.

Q> What's all this then, with these RAR archives, why not simply use ZIP ?
R> Evolution is required, else why not use windows 3.1 ( sorry for those who do) . WINRAR is much more efficient than WINZIP. With it's resizable dictionary (like the dos fat) and it's good compression rate, RAR compresses a 2MB text file to 160 kB, whereas ZIP only makes it to 400 kB, with an equal run time. That way, connection time is saved, and our web site's host transmission band is less required.
